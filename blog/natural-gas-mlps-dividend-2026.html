Key Numbers (2026): Natural gas MLPs trade at 6–9% distribution yields. Enterprise Products Partners (EPD): 6.8% yield, 27 consecutive years of distribution growth. Energy Transfer (ET): 8.1% yield, ~50,000 miles of pipeline. MPLX: 8.4% yield, midstream spine of Marathon Petroleum.

Natural gas MLPs are one of the most misunderstood corners of the income investing universe. The tax structure scares off most retail investors — K-1 forms, partnership accounting, UBTI rules — and that's precisely why the yields stay high. The market underpays for these assets because the paperwork is complicated.

Here's the core argument: North American natural gas infrastructure is essentially a regulated toll road. Every cubic foot of gas flowing through an Enterprise Products or Energy Transfer pipeline generates fee income. The underlying commodity price? Mostly irrelevant. What matters is volume throughput × fee rate. That's a fundamentally different risk profile than upstream oil & gas.

What Is a Natural Gas MLP?

A Master Limited Partnership (MLP) is a US publicly traded entity that passes nearly all income to "unitholders" rather than retaining it. The IRS classifies 90%+ of income as "qualifying income" (natural resource transportation and processing). This means the partnership itself pays no corporate income tax — the tax obligation flows through to unitholders.

For natural gas specifically, this applies to:

  • Interstate and intrastate pipeline transportation
  • Natural gas processing (separating NGLs — ethane, propane, butane — from raw gas)
  • Storage (underground caverns, LNG peak-shaving facilities)
  • Fractionation (splitting NGL mixtures into component parts)
  • Marine terminal export facilities (LNG export — growing segment 2026)
Marco's Thesis: The US LNG export boom (Sabine Pass, Corpus Christi, Freeport, Golden Pass — 14+ bcf/d online by 2027) structurally increases natural gas demand. Every bcf flowing to an export terminal first passes through a pipeline. MLP fee income follows volume, and volume is growing regardless of the gas spot price.

Enterprise Products Partners (EPD): The Gold Standard

EPD is the benchmark MLP. 27 consecutive years of distribution growth. Coverage ratio comfortably above 1.5x. Balance sheet: investment-grade rated (Baa1/BBB+), debt/EBITDA ~3.0x — conservative for a capital-intensive business. The founding Duncan family holds ~32% of units, directly aligning management with distribution stability.

EPD owns ~50,000 miles of pipeline, 14 deep-water docks, 23 natural gas processing plants, and storage capacity of ~160 MMBbls. Approximately 85% of revenues are fee-based with no direct commodity price exposure. The other 15% is commodity-linked (principally NGL marketing margins) — manageable.

Why EPD's Yield Stays High (6.8%)

The K-1 tax form. Most retail brokerage platforms handle K-1s poorly. IRA and 401k investors avoid MLPs due to UBTI (Unrelated Business Taxable Income) rules that can create phantom tax liability inside tax-exempt accounts. European investors face withholding complexity. The result: EPD trades at a structural discount to equivalent C-Corp pipeline companies.

For a taxable US account, EPD's distributions typically include a substantial return-of-capital (ROC) component — typically 50–80% — which reduces the cost basis rather than triggering immediate income tax. This defers taxation. For a German investor (Freistellungsauftrag), the tax treatment is more complicated — EPD often classified as a "Sonstige Einkünfte" rather than standard dividends, creating administrative overhead. That's precisely why I focus primarily on C-Corp alternatives like Enbridge or TC Energy for non-US portfolios.

Energy Transfer (ET): Yield Monster with Execution Risk

Energy Transfer pays ~8.1% — one of the highest yields in the US pipeline sector. The network spans ~125,000 miles across 44 US states, covering natural gas, NGL, crude oil, and refined products. The diversification is real. The leverage (Debt/EBITDA ~4.2x) is at the high end but manageable given the largely fee-based revenue model.

The risk: ET has a history of aggressive M&A (Enable Midstream 2021, Sunoco Logistics 2017, Williams Cos bid failed) and management decisions that have previously disadvantaged unitholders. The distribution was cut from $0.305 to $0.15 per quarter in 2020 (COVID), then reinstated and grown. That scar requires a risk premium in the yield — hence the ~8% vs EPD's ~7%.

My view: ET is fine for investors who understand the execution risk and are comfortable with the leverage profile. Not for conservative income mandates.

MPLX: The Hidden Gem

MPLX was spun out of Marathon Petroleum Corporation (MPC) and remains majority-owned by MPC (~64% of units). This creates an unusual dynamic: MPLX's customers are largely captive — Marathon's refineries ship crude through MPLX pipelines and process products through MPLX terminals. Volume is sticky by contract design.

Distribution coverage ratio ~1.5x. MPLX generated ~$5.6B of adjusted EBITDA in 2025, distributable cash flow comfortably covers the $3.7B in annual distributions. The 8.4% yield reflects the K-1 overhang, not fundamental business weakness. MPLX has steadily grown distributions (CAGR ~5% since IPO), making it compelling on a total-return basis for those willing to handle K-1 paperwork.

Dividend Safety: Coverage Ratio Is the Key Metric

For MLPs, the relevant metric is not the payout ratio (earnings-based, often misleading due to depreciation on long-lived assets). Instead:

Distribution Coverage Ratio = Distributable Cash Flow / Total Distributions Paid

Anything below 1.0x is a cut warning. 1.1–1.3x is acceptable. 1.5x+ is solid. EPD at 1.7x is near the upper bound for the sector — they retain excess cash for organic growth rather than paying it all out, which is conservative and distribution-protective. Understanding free cash flow mechanics is essential for evaluating any MLP.

What Can Cut a Distribution?

  • Volume collapse (a major customer goes bankrupt — Chesapeake 2020 being the classic example)
  • Leverage crisis (2020 COVID, some MLPs had debt/EBITDA >5x became unserviceable)
  • Regulatory / rate case decisions (FERC can reduce allowed returns on interstate pipelines)
  • Capital allocation failures (too much capex at poor returns, stranding distributable cash)
  • Commodity exposure if the MLP is not purely fee-based (gathering MLPs often have higher commodity risk)

The LNG Export Angle: Structural Tailwind for Natural Gas MLPs

The US became the world's largest LNG exporter in 2023. Capacity is expanding rapidly — Plaquemines LNG (Phase 1, 2025), Golden Pass LNG (2026), Corpus Christi Stage 3 (2025-2026), Port Arthur LNG (2027+). Each new export terminal requires dedicated pipeline feed gas supply contracts, typically 20-year offtake agreements.

Enterprise Products, Boardwalk Pipeline (private, Loews), and Kinder Morgan are the primary beneficiaries for interstate gas transport to Gulf Coast export hubs. The more LNG export capacity that comes online, the more pipeline throughput — and the more fee income for MLPs. This is a structural, multi-decade growth driver uncorrelated with short-term gas prices.

Marco's Take: I don't hold MLPs directly (K-1 complexity for a non-US resident). My pipeline exposure comes through Enbridge (ENB) and Kinder Morgan (KMI) — C-Corp structures with equivalent infrastructure quality and simpler tax treatment. For US investors comfortable with K-1 forms, EPD is arguably the highest-quality income instrument in North American infrastructure.

Checklist: Is a Natural Gas MLP Right for You?

  • Are you a US taxpayer comfortable with K-1 forms? (If not → consider C-Corp alternatives)
  • Do you hold in a taxable account? (IRA/401k → UBTI risk, consult advisor)
  • Is the distribution coverage ratio above 1.3x? (Below = higher cut risk)
  • Is debt/EBITDA below 4.5x? (Above = balance sheet risk in down cycles)
  • Does management have a track record of stable or growing distributions? (EPD 27yr streak vs ET's 2020 cut)
  • Is the MLP purely fee-based, or does it have commodity exposure? (Gathering MLPs = higher risk)

Natural Gas MLPs 2026: What's Changed

The first half of 2026 brought significant shifts to the MLP landscape. Kinder Morgan reported strong Q1 2026 results with management citing LNG export feed gas as the primary volume driver for 2026-2027 guidance. Energy Transfer (ET) continued its distribution growth path after the 2020 cut reset, now trading above pre-cut levels. Enterprise Products Partners reaffirmed its commitment to the 1.7x coverage target, maintaining its position as the gold standard for distribution safety in the sector.

Key 2026 Trend: AI data center power demand is becoming a genuine MLP tailwind. Natural gas is positioned as the primary baseload / backup power source for hyperscaler campuses in Texas, Oklahoma, and Pennsylvania — all states with dense MLP infrastructure. This is not a short-term thesis; data center power purchase agreements run 10–20 years, comparable to LNG offtake contracts. Pipeline operators with routes near data center clusters benefit directly.

MLP Tax Reform Watch: What a Potential K-1 Elimination Could Mean

Periodically, US lawmakers propose converting MLPs to C-Corp structures to simplify the K-1 burden that currently deters institutional ownership. If passed, such reform would dramatically expand the investor base for MLPs — driving multiple expansion similar to what happened with REITs in the 1990s. This is a long-tail optionality embedded in MLP ownership. It has been discussed without action for 15 years, but the trend is relevant. EPD and ET would be the most likely beneficiaries of a wider institutional investor base.

MLP Distribution Reinvestment: DRIP in Pipeline Investments

Most MLPs do not offer traditional DRIP programs (Dividend Reinvestment Plans) directly — primarily because their limited partnership structure and K-1 complexity makes automatic share reinvestment logistically difficult at the broker level. However, there are practical alternatives:

  • DRIP via broker (C-Corp only): Stocks like Kinder Morgan (KMI) and ONEOK (OKE) — organized as C-Corps rather than partnerships — offer standard 1099-DIV treatment and full DRIP compatibility at most brokers. Enbridge (ENB) likewise supports DRIP directly through its transfer agent.
  • Manual reinvestment (MLP route): For true MLPs (EPD, ET, MPLX), most investors receive quarterly cash distributions and must reinvest manually. Use the DRIP Calculator to model the long-term compounding impact of reinvesting MLP distributions.
  • DRIP simulation: Even without a formal DRIP, you can simulate the effect by reinvesting each distribution quarterly. At a 7% yield with 3% distribution growth, the compounding effect over 15 years nearly triples the original income stream.

Natural Gas Infrastructure 2026–2030: Long-Term Structural Thesis

The long-term case for natural gas MLPs rests on three structural pillars that go beyond near-term pricing cycles:

  1. LNG Export Buildout: US LNG export capacity is projected to double by 2030 (from ~14 Bcf/d to ~28 Bcf/d). Every LNG export terminal needs domestic feed gas — routed through existing pipeline infrastructure. This creates multi-decade volume visibility for trunk line operators.
  2. Power Grid Electrification: As the US expands wind and solar capacity, natural gas fills the intermittency gap. Grid operators need reliable dispatchable generation — gas turbines remain the primary solution. More electricity demand from data centers, heat pumps, and EVs means more gas demand for power generation through 2035.
  3. Mexico Export Growth: Texas-Mexico cross-border gas exports grew +8% in 2025 and are expected to accelerate. Southern natural gas pipelines benefit disproportionately from this structural trend.

The structural thesis supports distribution stability — and potentially growth — even in scenarios where domestic residential gas demand declines modestly. Investors should focus on fee-based operators with LNG and power-sector exposure, not commodity-price-sensitive gathering MLPs.
