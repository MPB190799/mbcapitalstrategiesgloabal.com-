Hard Asset Market Update KW42 — Gold Corrects, Brent Holds, VIX at 13

Date: October 16, 2026 | Series: Market Update | Ticker: —

---

The hard asset market shows a mixed picture in week 42: gold has retreated from its September highs of $2,030 to $1,875/oz (-7.6%), while Brent crude oil remains stable at $84.50/barrel and the VIX sits at 12.97 — historically low. The DAX trades at 13,450 points, the S&P 500 at 4,851 — both indices remain in their yearly uptrends without meaningful corrections.

#

Gold: Pulling Back from September Highs to $1,875

Gold has continued its correction after reaching a yearly high of $2,030 in September (source: World Gold Council), now trading at $1,875 per ounce — a decline of roughly 7.6% from peak. This pullback is normal within the broader 2026 context: the average annual price was $1,797/oz with a low of $1,649 in January and the aforementioned September high.

What's behind it: The gold correction follows a classic pattern after major central bank rate decisions. In September 2026, the ECB, Fed, and BoJ all raised their key rates — responding to persistent inflation pressures in services sectors. Higher interest rates increase the opportunity cost of holding gold (zero yield), prompting institutional investors to trim positions.

#

Brent Crude: Stable at $84.50/Barrel

Brent crude is trading at $84.50 per barrel, showing remarkable stability despite geopolitical tensions around the Strait of Hormuz. This price sits in the upper third of its 12-month range and reflects the tension between supply risks and demand-side concerns.

Supply side: Hormuz tensions remain a latent risk — approximately 21% of global oil transit passes through this chokepoint (source: EIA). Any escalation would immediately push prices higher. Simultaneously, OPEC+ production limits keep supply controlled, creating a natural price floor.

#

VIX at 12.97: Low Volatility — Opportunity or Danger?

The VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) sits at 12.97 — well below its long-term average of roughly 20.

Historical context: When the VIX stays below 15 for extended periods, statistics show volatility tends to increase within 3–6 months — not because markets must crash, but because low option premiums tempt traders into taking more risk. This can then trigger larger moves in either direction.

#

DAX and S&P 500: Yearly Trends Intact

The DAX closed at 13,450 points, the S&P 500 at 4,851. Both indices are trading in their respective uptrends without meaningful deviations from long-term moving averages.

#

Outlook Weeks 42/43

Gold: $1,800–$1,850 is the next support zone. Brent: As long as Hormuz doesn't escalate and OPEC+ stays disciplined, $80–$90 is the operating band. VIX below 15 = favorable environment for position-building, but no free pass for leverage. Q4 earnings season begins — first quarterly reports from energy and materials sectors arrive in coming weeks.
