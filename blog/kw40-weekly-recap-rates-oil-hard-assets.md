## The Week in Numbers

US equity markets closed KW40 slightly negative: the S&P 500 lost 1.26% to 7,666 points, the Dow Jones gave up 1.81% (50,927), and the NASDAQ fell 1.37% to 26,872. Data as of Friday, October 2 — before Germany's bridge weekend.

What do these corrections mean? Not much on their own. But in context: the US 10-year yield rose 5.43% to **5.24%** — the highest level in months. And that is precisely the driving force behind movements across hard assets.

The mechanism is straightforward: when risk-free paper yields over 5%, gold must offer a higher opportunity-cost premium to compete.

### Volatility Rises

The VIX climbed 13.6% to 16.14 — not panic levels, but a clear rise from the low range. Markets are pricing in Q3 earnings uncertainty and the question of whether September's Fed hike is only the first step.

## Shipping: Tanker Stocks Run Against the Current

While gold mining came under pressure (Newmont -9.89%, Barrick -5.73%), the shipping sector proved resilient: **Frontline rose 7.55% to $51.54** during KW40.

Why this divergence? Tanker cash flows are driven primarily by freight rates and order book positions — not by interest rate levels. Current VLCC rates sit in the upper quartile of the cycle, and most major tanker companies are debt-free or have long-term charter contracts with fixed rates.

This is the difference between cash-flow-driven and rate-sensitive assets: when the mechanism behind the price doesn't depend on interest rates, higher rates pass through the sector — sometimes even profitably.

---

## Mining: Gold Price Decline Hits Producers Harder

Newmont (-9.89%) and Barrick (-5.73%) lost significantly more than gold itself (-3.65%). This is typical for mining stocks in a downtrend: they move with higher beta than the underlying commodity. The reason lies in margins — when gold prices fall, free cash flow margins compress faster than linearly because production costs are relatively fixed.

For cyclical investors this means: mining stocks are not a pure gold proxy. They carry their own leverage that works both on the upside and downside.

## Outlook KW41: Q3 Earnings Begin

Starting KW41, US companies begin Q3 pre-announcements. For hard assets these are particularly relevant:
- **Shipping:** Tanker Q3 will show whether high rates translated into cash flow
- **Mining:** Gold producers must prove that cost pressure below $4,000/oz remains manageable
- **Upstream Oil & Gas:** Brent above $100 should deliver strong quarters — but guidance matters more than retro

The Fed hike to 3.75-4.00% on September 17 continues to reverberate: higher yields press on everything not self-financed from cash flow. For hard assets with their own cash flow and low debt, the thesis remains intact — but the valuation correction is real.
