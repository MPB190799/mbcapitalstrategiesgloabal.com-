KI Trade 2026: Transformers, Grids & the Best Stocks

Published 12 October 2026 · Stock Analysis Series · ETN VRT HUBB

The AI revolution has a bottleneck — and it's not chips. It's power infrastructure. While Nvidia and AMD compete for GPU capacity, a quiet shortage is emerging in transformers, switchgear, and cooling systems. Lead times in the US have reached 18–24 months (Bonneville Power Administration). This isn't short-term hype — it's a structural bottleneck with cyclical leverage.

The Mechanism: Why Grids Are the Real AI Theme

The IEA estimates in its report "Data Centres and AI Technologies" (June 2024) that global data center electricity consumption will rise from ~480 TWh in 2022 to between 1,300–2,000 TWh by the end of this decade. In the US alone, roughly 140 GW of additional capacity is needed by 2030 — equivalent to approximately Germany's entire current electricity generation.

Each Nvidia H100 GPU consumes ~700 watts — double its predecessor A100 (~350W). In clusters with tens of thousands of chips, a modern AI data center draws as much power as a mid-sized city. And this power must flow through transformers and distribution networks — components whose production capacities have been shrinking for years because the market was considered "saturated".

The result: Demand is exploding, supply is rigid. Whoever builds this infrastructure profits regardless of whether OpenAI or Google wins AI dominance.

Eaton Corp (ETN) — $429.65 (+1.21%)

Eaton is the largest US manufacturer of transformers and switchgear. Its Electrical Grids segment grew organically by 27% in Q3 FY26 — driven exclusively by transformer demand from data center and renewable construction. Total revenue came in at $4.8B (+19% YoY), EPS of $1.54 beat consensus of $1.48. Guidance for FY26 calls for 10–13% growth ($18.3–18.6B revenue).

Why this matters: Eaton has massively expanded its production capacities since 2022 — new plants in Ohio and Indiana are online. This means lead times are slowly decreasing, but revenue continues to grow because demand is rising faster than supply catches up. The P/E sits at ~35x (premium valuation), justified by the +19% organic growth curve.

Sources: Eaton Q3 FY26 Earnings Release; Bloomberg Consensus Estimates

Vertiv Holdings (VRT) — $242.78 (-0.39%)

Vertiv specializes in power distribution and cooling within data centers. The company delivers the infrastructure between the grid connection and server racks: UPS systems, precision cooling, rack power distribution units. Revenue grew 15% YoY to $2.7B in Q2 FY26, with the data center segment expanding organically by 18%.

The key point: Vertiv's backlog sits at ~$9–10 billion — a record high. This means the next 12–18 quarters are already planned today. At a P/E of ~65x, the valuation is elevated, but the backlog gives the growth curve rare visibility.

Sources: Vertiv Q2 FY26 Earnings Release; Bloomberg Consensus Estimates

Hubbell Incorporated (HUBB) — $847.30 (+0.52%)

Hubbell is a smaller but equally relevant player in electrical infrastructure, with strong exposure to utility-scale transformers and grid modernization. The company's Electrical Distribution segment benefited directly from the US CHIPS Act funding for domestic manufacturing of power equipment. Revenue grew 14% YoY in Q3 FY26.

The angle: Hubbell trades at a more moderate P/E (~28x) compared to Eaton and Vertiv, offering exposure to the same theme with lower valuation risk. The company has been acquiring strategically — adding capabilities in switchgear and metering that complement its transformer business.

Sources: Hubbell Q3 FY26 Earnings Release; Bloomberg Consensus Estimates

The Cyclical Lens: What Could Go Wrong?

Three risks to monitor:

1. Supply catch-up. As new transformer plants come online (Eaton's Ohio/Indiana, GE Vernova's expansions), lead times will normalize. The margin premium from scarcity pricing will compress — this is the classic cyclical inflection point.

2. AI capex slowdown. If hyperscalers reduce data center spending in 2027 (as some analysts predict post-2026 build-out), transformer demand could decelerate faster than expected.

3. Valuation risk. At current multiples, these stocks price in years of sustained growth. A single quarter of disappointment would trigger meaningful drawdowns — especially VRT at ~65x P/E.

Verdict

The power infrastructure theme is real and structurally driven — not a narrative trade. The question isn't whether transformers are needed, but how long the supply gap persists. For cyclical investors: the window to participate at reasonable valuations may be narrowing as these names have already rallied significantly in 2025–2026.

Key takeaway: The AI trade isn't over — it's just moving downstream from chips to cables, transformers, and cooling. And that shift favors companies with order backlogs, not just growth stories.
