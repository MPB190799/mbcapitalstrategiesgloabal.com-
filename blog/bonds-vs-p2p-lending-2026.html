Bonds vs P2P Lending: Which Fits a Hard-Asset Income Portfolio?

Published: November 20, 2026 · By Marco Bozem · Not financial advice.

Bonds or P2P lending — which earns its yield in 2026?
November 2026 snapshot: IG corporates ~5.15–5.35%, high-yield ~6.9–7.3%, P2P platforms 5–12% with Debitum averaging ~8.98% on secured SME loans. The extra 2 points of P2P yield over HY bonds are payment for credit and liquidity risk you carry yourself. Bonds are the liquid, rated ballast; P2P is the higher-yield satellite with weak secondary liquidity.

The 2026 income landscape: what do bonds pay right now?

Fact: the income backdrop shifted in late 2026. The Fed delivered a widely expected rate cut at its October meeting, and officials signaled two additional cuts are possible before mid-2027 — although a hotter-than-expected Q3 GDP print of 2.8% has some economists expecting a slower easing path. As of early November, the numbers look like this:

Fixed income, early November 2026 (market data)
  • 10-year Treasury: ~4.12–4.15%, down about 25 bps from September highs
  • Investment-grade corporates: ~5.15–5.35% — spreads of ~100–120 bps, near the tight end of the 10-year range
  • High-yield corporates: ~6.9–7.3% — spreads of ~280–310 bps, historically tight
  • Implication: most corporate yield is spread, and strategists see limited room for further compression

Read that table as a risk-price list. At 4.1% the Treasury pays you for duration, not for credit. At 5.2% the IG market pays you about 100–120 bps for corporate credit risk — historically little. At ~7% the HY market pays you ~300 bps for default risk, also historically tight. In other words: the bond market is not generous in 2026; it is fully priced. New-issue books have been heavily oversubscribed, which is exactly what a fully-priced market looks like from the buy side.

What does P2P lending pay — and what backs the yield?

Fact: peer-to-peer lending platforms broadly quote returns in the 5–12% range, with most investors earning between 5% and 8% depending on platform and risk class. Individual higher-risk loans run 10–16%. On the secured-business-loan side, Debitum — a platform financing European SMEs — reports an average annual return of 8.98% across its investor base, with loans currently yielding around 9%, backed by secured loans to small and medium-sized companies. Note the caveat the platform itself carries: returns have moderated somewhat as European rates declined.

What backs that extra yield? Three things, none of them free:

  • Credit risk on small borrowers: SME loans default more than IG corporates, full stop. The 2–3 points over HY bonds are compensation for borrower-level risk you assess yourself (or trust the platform's originators to assess)
  • Liquidity risk: bonds trade daily; most P2P loans lock you in until maturity. Exit markets exist on some platforms but are thin, and selling early usually means accepting a discount
  • Platform risk: you hold a claim serviced by a platform and its originators — an operational layer that simply does not exist in a Treasury or an ETF

Risk, side by side: duration vs default vs liquidity?

The honest comparison is not yield vs yield — it is which risk you'd rather own:

Bonds vs P2P — the risk matrix (facts + my reading)
  • Bonds: daily liquidity, ratings, index diversification; main risks are duration and spread compression (spreads are already tight)
  • P2P: no daily liquidity, no ratings, loan-by-loan diversification; main risks are borrower default, platform health, and thin secondary markets
  • Correlation: P2P SME credit correlates with the economic cycle — in a recession, defaults rise exactly when you need the cash; IG bonds rally on the same scenario (flight to quality)
  • My reading: in a rate-cut cycle with tight spreads, adding P2P is a credit-and-liquidity bet, not a free lunch

That last row is the one most yield-seekers skip. In the next recession, your HY bonds will fall in price but likely recover; your P2P loans will simply default at a higher rate, and the platform's recovery process will take years, not quarters. The two instruments are not substitutes — they respond differently to the same shock.

Which fits a hard-asset income portfolio?

My thesis (not a fact): for a hard-asset income investor, bonds are ballast and P2P is a satellite. Here's the logic. A hard-asset portfolio already carries deep cyclicality through shipping freight rates and commodity prices — the income stream itself is volatile. Adding P2P on top stacks a second, uncorrelated-on-paper but practically correlated credit exposure with no liquidity. What the portfolio actually needs is a liquid, rate-sensitive asset that rises in the downturn: that's what Treasuries and IG bonds do.

If you do add P2P, size it as satellite: my working rule (thesis) is a small single-digit percentage of the income stack, spread across many small loans and at least one platform, with the expectation that access to your money is the price of the extra yield. The 8.98% average Debitum reports is real published data — but it is an average across a portfolio you don't control, earned on loans you can't sell.

A concrete illustration (my own arithmetic): on a $10,000 income stack — $4,000 in IG bonds at ~5.2%, $3,000 in HY at ~7.1%, $3,000 in P2P at ~9% — gross income lands near $691 per year, a blended ~6.9%. Swap the P2P slice into IG and the blend drops to ~6.3%: you're being paid ~$60/year extra for carrying SME default and platform risk with no liquidity. Whether that's worth it is a risk-appetite question, not a math question. The honest verdict after the cut?

The Fed's October cut made income investing marginally easier and marginally more crowded. Bonds at 4.1–5.3% are fairly priced, not cheap; P2P at 5–12% offers a genuine yield premium priced in genuine risks. My bottom line: hold the liquid, rated ballast in bonds; if you reach for P2P, reach deliberately — small size, many loans, eyes open about liquidity — and never let a satellite position determine whether your income plan survives a recession.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are P2P loans better than bonds?

Neither is categorically better — they price different risks. In November 2026, IG corporate bonds yield roughly 5.15–5.35% and high-yield bonds 6.9–7.3%, both with daily liquidity and ratings; P2P platforms quote 5–12% with Debitum averaging about 8.98% on secured SME loans, but with little or no secondary liquidity and platform risk. P2P pays roughly 2 points over HY bonds for credit and liquidity risk you carry yourself. Bonds are the liquid ballast; P2P is a satellite position, sized small if used at all.

What do corporate bonds yield in 2026?

As of early November 2026: the 10-year Treasury trades around 4.12–4.15% after the Fed's October rate cut, investment-grade corporate bonds yield approximately 5.15–5.35% (spreads of about 100–120 bps, near the tight end of the 10-year range), and high-yield bonds yield roughly 6.9–7.3% with spreads around 280–310 bps — historically tight, which is why strategists see limited room for further spread compression.

Is P2P lending safe during a recession?

No — it is pro-cyclical. SME borrower defaults rise in a recession exactly when you may need the cash, and the thin secondary markets on P2P platforms mean exiting early usually means accepting a discount. Bonds behave differently: IG bonds typically rally on flight to quality during the same downturn. If you hold P2P, treat it as a small satellite allocation spread across many loans, and never count it as your emergency liquidity.
