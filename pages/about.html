My Investor Journey: From Growth-Stock Pain to a Hard-Asset Strategy

2022: Getting Started — and Getting Burned

In 2022 I started investing systematically — like most people at first: ETFs, growth stocks, tech hype. Then interest rates rose sharply and growth stocks crashed. My portfolio crashed with them. That was the turning point: I no longer wanted to depend on price appreciation. I wanted companies that pay me cash every quarter, regardless of what the market does.

2023: Discovering Hard Assets

The search for real, physically secured cashflow led me to hard assets: shipping stocks (tankers earn as long as crude oil is transported), midstream pipelines (operators collect throughput fees regardless of oil prices), and mining (producers and royalty companies that turn real commodities into dividends). These business models pay distributions from genuine economic activity, not accounting tricks.

2024: Building the Platform & Research Community

I began sharing my research on YouTube — and quickly noticed many investors ask the same question: “How do I find stocks that pay truly sustainable dividends?” MB Capital Strategies grew out of that: a platform with real analyses, real numbers, real positions. No buy recommendations — just research.

2025–2026: Scaling & Deepening

Today the portfolio spans roughly 277 positions, heavily weighted toward shipping (CMB.Tech, TORM, Dorian LPG, FLEX LNG), with meaningful allocations in mining (Thungela, Vale), energy (Ecopetrol, BP, APA) and midstream (ONEOK, Enagás). As a contributor on Investing.com, I also reach an international investor audience.

My Approach: Cashflow Over Capital Gains

Most retail investors chase price appreciation. I invest differently: cashflow first. Dividends from hard assets — from companies that move real, physical goods — form the core of my portfolio.

Why Hard Assets?

Energy gets transported, commodities get mined, ships sail — regardless of what Mr. Market thinks on any given day. This economic reality provides inflation protection and delivers predictable distributions. My Hard Assets Guide explains the four core categories with concrete metrics.

The 80/20 Rule

80% of my focus goes to stable, predictable dividend payers — midstream pipelines, infrastructure, mining producers. 20% goes to more cyclical high-dividend positions like tanker & shipping stocks, which deliver outsized returns during up-cycles but require careful cycle awareness.
