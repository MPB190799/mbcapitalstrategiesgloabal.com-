How I Research Dividend Stocks: My Methodology and Standards

Updated November 26, 2026 · By Marco Bozem, private investor · Not financial advice

This site is written by one person: me, Marco Bozem, a private investor from Germany who has been investing since 2022 with a focus on dividends from hard assets (shipping, energy, mining and infrastructure). This page explains how the research is done, so you can judge how much weight to give it.

1. Where the numbers come from

  • Company filings first. Annual and quarterly reports, earnings releases, investor presentations, and filings with the SEC (10-K, 20-F, 6-K) or the relevant exchange (for example Oslo Børs, Euronext, the LSE or the JSE). If a number in an article describes a company, it should come from the company itself.
  • Official sources for rules. Tax and regulatory statements come from tax authorities, laws or well-known tax advisory publications, not from forum posts.
  • Market data (prices, yields) comes from standard data providers and is always dated, because it changes daily.
  • My own portfolio data comes from my real broker accounts, tracked in Parqet. When I write "I received" or "I paid", that is from those records.

2. Facts and opinions are kept apart

A reader should always be able to tell what is a reported fact and what is my view. Reported figures are attributed to their source and dated. My own assessments are written as such ("my view", "my thesis", "I would"). Worked examples and projections state their assumptions and are labelled as illustrations.

3. What I look at in a dividend stock

  1. Is the dividend covered? Payout ratio on the company's own cash measure: free cash flow, distributable earnings (mortgage REITs) or AFFO (REITs).
  2. What is the payout rule? A fixed amount per share, or a percentage of profit as in most shipping companies. See how variable payout formulas work.
  3. Balance sheet. Net debt, maturities, loan-to-value. Debt competes with the dividend for the same cash.
  4. Cycle position. For commodities and shipping: supply (order books, fleet age, mine pipelines) and where rates or prices are relative to history.
  5. Net yield after tax. Domicile and withholding tax matter. My own numbers are in what I keep from foreign dividends.
  6. Warning signs. The checklist in dividend traps I didn't avoid, written after two expensive mistakes.

4. Conflicts of interest

  • I own many of the stocks I write about. My holdings are listed on the portfolio page, losers included. Articles mention when I hold a position.
  • Affiliate links (for example to a research tool or broker) are marked, and I may earn a commission. Details in the affiliate disclosure.
  • No paid articles. Companies cannot buy an analysis or a rating on this site. Any sponsored content would be labelled as an advertisement.

5. How AI tools are used

I use software, including AI tools, to collect data, draft structure and translate between German and English (much of my work is also published in German on mbcapitalstrategies.com). I decide the topics, the positions and the conclusions. Figures are checked against the primary sources listed above before an article is published. If you find something that looks machine-written and wrong, I want to know.

6. Updates and corrections

Articles carry a publication date; market data in them is valid as of that date. When I find an error, I correct it and, if it changes the conclusion, note the correction in the article. You can report errors through the contact page.

7. What this site is not

It is not a licensed advisory service, and it doesn't know your situation. I am not authorised by any financial regulator. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell; it is how one private investor thinks about his own money, shown openly.
