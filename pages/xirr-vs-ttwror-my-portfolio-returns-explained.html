17.5% or 10.2% a Year? Why My Portfolio Has Two Different Returns

November 26, 2026 · By Marco Bozem, private investor · Not financial advice

On my portfolio page I show two return figures from Parqet, and they look contradictory: an XIRR of 17.5% a year and a TTWROR of 57.8% since March 2022. Spread over the 4.7 years since I started, that 57.8% works out to about 10.2% a year. Both numbers are correct. They answer different questions, and anyone who quotes a single return figure should know which one they're quoting.

What each number measures

  • XIRR (money-weighted): the internal rate of return of all my actual cash flows: every deposit, every dividend, today's value. Periods in which a lot of money was invested count more than periods in which little was invested. It answers: what did my money actually earn?
  • TTWROR (true time-weighted rate of return): splits the history into sub-periods between cash flows and chains their returns. Deposits and withdrawals have no influence. It answers: how well did the investments perform, regardless of when I added money? This is how funds and indices report returns.

A simple example

Year 1: you invest €1,000 and it earns 0%. At the start of year 2 you add €9,000, and in year 2 everything earns 20%. At the end you have €12,000.

Time-weighted: 0% in year 1, 20% in year 2 → 20% over two years, about 9.5% a year.
Money-weighted (XIRR): most of the money was only invested in the good year → about 17.9% a year.
My own calculation; illustrative numbers.

The investments performed the same in both views. The difference comes entirely from when the money was there.

Why my XIRR is higher

My portfolio looks a lot like that example. It was small in 2022 and 2023 and grew mainly later, as I kept investing new money. You can see the growth in my dividends: €437 in 2023, €4,646 in 2025 (details in my dividend income 2022–2026). At the same time, 2026 was a very strong year for the tanker and energy stocks that make up a large part of the portfolio. More money in the good years pushes the XIRR above the time-weighted return.

The flip side: the same effect works in reverse. If the large, recently invested part of the portfolio has a bad year, the XIRR will fall faster than the time-weighted return.

Which number I'd quote

  • To compare with an index or a fund: the time-weighted figure, about 10.2% a year. That's the like-for-like comparison.
  • To say what my money earned: the XIRR of 17.5% a year.
  • Always with the period and the source. Both are calculated by Parqet from my real transactions across three brokers, from 9 March 2022 to 26 November 2026. Past returns are no guarantee for the future.

A portfolio that only shows its XIRR can look better than its investments really were, and one that only shows its time-weighted return hides what the owner actually earned. Showing both is the honest version, which is why my portfolio page lists both.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between XIRR and TTWROR?

XIRR is a money-weighted return: periods in which more money was invested count more. TTWROR (true time-weighted rate of return) removes the effect of deposits and withdrawals and measures how each euro invested performed over time.

Why is my XIRR higher than my time-weighted return?

Usually because more money was invested during the periods with the best returns. Then the money-weighted return is pulled up, while the time-weighted return treats every period equally.

Which return should I compare with an index?

The time-weighted return, because index and fund returns are calculated time-weighted too. The XIRR is better for answering what your own money actually earned.
