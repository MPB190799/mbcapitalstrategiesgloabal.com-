What drove the growth

Two things move dividend income: how much money is invested, and how much that money pays. My numbers contain both, and I haven't separated them here. Over these years I kept investing new money, and in 2025 and 2026 a significant part of the portfolio sat in shipping stocks during an unusually strong freight market. Those companies pay a share of their profits, so their dividends rose with rates (I explain the mechanics in how shipping payout formulas work).

That second point matters for reading the chart honestly. Part of the jump from 2024 to 2026 is cyclical. In a weaker freight market, the same shipping positions would pay noticeably less. I would not extrapolate the 2025 to 2026 growth rate into the future.

Why my tax rate went up from 5% to 26%

This is the part I found most instructive. In Germany, everyone has a tax-free allowance of €1,000 per year on all investment income combined. Above it, dividends are taxed at a flat 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge), and foreign withholding tax is credited only up to the treaty rate.

  • In 2022 and 2024 the allowance covered much of my income, so foreign withholding made up most of the tax. The tax share stayed low (5.4% and 11.8%).
  • In 2023 the share was higher (15.4%) than in 2024 even though income was smaller. The mix of payers and their countries changes from year to year, and so does the rate; I haven't broken this down further.
  • From 2025 on, income was far above €1,000, and the rate moved towards the full flat rate: 21.1% in 2025 and 26.1% in 2026.

The practical lesson: early on, dividend income looks almost tax-free, but that changes once it outgrows the allowance. Anyone planning to live from dividends should plan with roughly three quarters of the gross amount, not the full figure. Where this leaves me

Over the last twelve months the portfolio paid me €4,922 after tax, about €410 a month. The portfolio is worth about €120,700, and since March 2022 it has returned 17.5% a year according to Parqet's money-weighted calculation (XIRR).

Frequently asked questions

How much dividend income does Marco Bozem receive?

According to his broker records, €6,083 gross and €4,490 net from 1 January to 26 November 2026, and €4,922 net over the twelve months to 26 November 2026.

Why does the tax rate on dividends rise as income grows in Germany?

Every person in Germany has a €1,000 annual tax-free allowance on investment income. While dividends are small, most of them fall under it. Once income is far above the allowance, the effective rate approaches the flat 26.375% (plus foreign withholding that is not credited).
