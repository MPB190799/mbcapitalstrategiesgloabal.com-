Reclaiming Withholding Tax from Belgium, Norway and Denmark: How It Works and When It's Worth It

November 26, 2026 · By Marco Bozem, private investor · Not financial advice

In my 2026 tax numbers three countries stood out: Belgium, Norway and Denmark withhold more tax at source than Germany credits. The excess is not lost forever; each country has a refund procedure. The question I wanted to answer for myself is a practical one: how much money is actually at stake in my portfolio, and is the paperwork worth it?

So the whole question is worth about €76 for this year, and more than 80% of that comes from one stock. That already shows where the money is.

Belgium: form 276 Div.-Aut.

Belgium withholds 30% on dividends. The treaty with Germany limits Belgian tax to 15% for private investors, and Germany only credits those 15%. The other 15 points can be reclaimed with the Belgian form 276 Div.-Aut., which the Belgian federal finance administration publishes together with explanatory notes. The beneficial owner fills it in, the home tax authority confirms residence, and proof of the dividend payment (the broker's dividend statement) is attached.

Is it worth it for me? About €61 for 2026. That's a real amount, and CMB.Tech is my largest position, so it repeats every year. The effort is one form per claim plus a confirmation from my local tax office. At this size the effort pays off, especially when a full year of payments is bundled into one claim.

Norway: RF-1534 via Altinn

Norwegian companies deduct 25% withholding tax unless they know the shareholder qualifies for a lower rate. Shareholders resident in a treaty country can apply for a refund online with form RF-1534 through Norway's Altinn portal. According to the Norwegian Tax Administration, you can only apply after the company's correction deadline has passed, usually about four months after payment, and the deadline is five years from the end of the year in which you received the dividend.

Is it worth it for me? €9.46 for 2026. Because the deadline is generous, the sensible approach is to let several years accumulate and file once, if the position grows. For a single year at this size, the effort is hard to justify.

Denmark: online claim with Skattestyrelsen

Danish companies withhold 27% dividend tax. Under the treaty, private investors resident in Germany end up at 15%, so 12 points are refundable. Claims go through the Danish Tax Agency's online form; up to 20 dividend payments of one shareholder fit into one claim, and you can apply with or without a Danish MitID login. Denmark has tightened its documentation requirements after a large refund-fraud scandal, and a certificate of tax residence is generally required.

Is it worth it for me? €5.63 for 2026. Not worth filing on its own. The more useful lesson is for buying decisions: at small position sizes, a Danish dividend stock starts with a permanent 12-point handicap unless the investor files regularly.

What I take from this

  • Size decides. The procedures are similar in effort whether you reclaim €5 or €500. Reclaims make sense for large positions in high-withholding countries, and hardly at all for small ones.
  • Check before you buy, not after. The cheapest fix is choosing a company domiciled in a country with low or no withholding, if two investments are otherwise comparable. More on that in how shipping dividends are taxed.
  • Keep the dividend statements. Every procedure needs proof of what was paid and withheld. Downloading the broker statements once a year makes any later claim much easier.

Procedures and forms change. Check the current requirements with the Belgian Federal Public Service Finance, the Norwegian Tax Administration (Skatteetaten) and the Danish Tax Agency (Skattestyrelsen) before filing. This is my own research as a private investor, not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can a German investor get back the excess Belgian withholding tax?

Belgium withholds 30% on dividends, while the treaty rate for German residents is 15%. The difference can be claimed with Belgian form 276 Div.-Aut., which the home tax office confirms, together with proof of the dividend payment.

How do I reclaim Norwegian withholding tax?

Norway withholds 25% by default. Shareholders resident in a treaty country can apply online with form RF-1534 via Altinn. Norway's tax administration says the deadline is five years from the end of the year in which the dividend was received.

How do I reclaim Danish dividend tax?

Denmark withholds 27%. The refund down to the treaty rate is claimed with Skattestyrelsen's online form; up to 20 dividend payments of one shareholder can be included per claim.
